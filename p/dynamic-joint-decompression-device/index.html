Technical Description – Dynamic Joint Decompression Device

This project involves the development of a patented mechanical device that reduces compression on a joint while preserving limb mobility.

The system’s development required several design iterations and the exploration of different mechanisms before arriving at the current architecture. These prototyping and testing phases identified the limitations of the initial approaches and guided the design toward a more robust and functional solution. The integration of technology based on flexible fiberglass rods was a key advancement in the project and made the device viable.

The system uses fiberglass rods arranged in a matrix to increase their flexibility and adaptability to movement. These rods are attached to one end of the device and inserted into a mechanical spring that acts as a force-generating element.

A motor or activation mechanism applies force to the spring to compress it. This compression generates an opposing force, producing a decompression effect at the targeted joint.

At the other end, the fiberglass rods remain free to slide, allowing the system to adapt to changes in limb position during movement. The spring thus exerts a constant decompression force, while the flexible rods maintain the mechanism’s alignment while allowing joint movement.

This design combines mechanical decompression assistance with a wide range of motion. The developed technology enabled the project to be realized and is now being used by a company in the development of joint assistance solutions.

These versions allowed for the validation of mechanical principles, the testing of various configurations, and the optimization of the system’s behavior. They represent versions prior to the final design and contributed to the completion of the first functional version of the device.

Project Description – Spinal Unloading Exoskeleton

This project involves the development of a mechanical exoskeleton designed to redirect some of the loads applied to the spine and shoulders to the hips. The device aims to reduce mechanical stress on the upper body while maintaining natural freedom of movement.

The main structure consists of a series of artificial vertebrae made of plastic. These vertebrae are aligned to replicate the kinematics of the human spine.

Each vertebra has a central opening for the insertion of a fiberglass rod. This rod acts as a structural guide while allowing the vertebrae to move freely along its axis. This system ensures both alignment and the transmission of mechanical forces to the hips.

The vertebrae are connected by a system of high-capacity elastic bands that maintain compression between the elements. This elastic preload allows the system to remain compact while facilitating movement.

When a user leans forward or bends, the elastic bands stretch, allowing the vertebrae to separate slightly and follow the curve of the movement. When the posture returns to the initial position, the tension in the elastic bands automatically brings the vertebrae back into contact, thus restoring the system’s original configuration.

This mechanism therefore combines:

load transfer to the hips

biomechanical flexibility

passive return to the neutral position

Together, it creates an articulated structure capable of supporting the body’s movements while helping to reduce stress on the spine and shoulders.
